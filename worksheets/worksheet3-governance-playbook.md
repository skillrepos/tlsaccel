# Worksheet 3 — One-Page Governance Playbook
*Enterprise Leader AI Accelerator · take-home (no lab fills it in)*

**What this is:** a one-page **governance playbook**: seven boxes that say how AI is used where you work, from what is in use today to what is kept on record. It is for back at work: every box is **Optional**, and no lab step asks you to fill it in. On Worksheet 4 (Lab 8) you set the date your usage policy (Box 2) goes out and, if you have time, a decide-by date for Boxes 3–7. Each box says what goes in it and gives an example from a made-up company.

Fill it in on the worksheets page, which saves what you type on your own device: https://skillrepos.github.io/tlsaccel/#ws3

## The seven boxes

All seven boxes are for back at work. Worksheet 4 holds the dates: when the inventory (Box 1) starts, when the usage policy (Box 2) goes out, and a decide-by date for each of Boxes 3–7.

### Back at work: Box 1 — Inventory (where is AI used today?)

**Optional, for back at work.** List two or three AI tools already in use where you work. For each one, give the **tool and what it is used for**, its **owner** (the one person answerable for it; "nobody yet" is a real answer), and the **data it touches** (what information it can see). **Autonomy tier** is how much the tool does on its own. **1 assist:** AI drafts, a person does the work. **2 review:** AI prepares the action, a named person approves it. **3 autonomous:** AI acts, and people check afterwards.

**Keep it general.** Give owners as job titles, not names. Describe the kind of data ("customer emails"), never the data itself. Describe unapproved use as a habit ("people paste customer emails into personal chatbots"), without saying who does it. No lab step asks you to paste this box into an AI tool, so do not.

### Back at work: Box 2 — Usage policy headlines (three rules you'd publish this week)

A usage policy is the short list of rules for using AI at work. Write three of those rules as headlines: one line each, plain enough to follow without asking anyone.

| Box | Needed in the lab? | What goes in it | Example | Your answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rule 1** | Optional | One rule in one line, for example about which AI tools people may use | Use the tools on the approved list. Anything else needs a yes from the review group first. | |

## Back at work: Boxes 3–7: the rest of the playbook

In Lab 8, if you have time, you give each of these five boxes a **decide-by date** on Worksheet 4: the date by which you will have settled it. Fill in the boxes themselves back at work.

### Back at work: Box 3 — Approval path

The approval path is how a new AI idea gets a yes or a no: it is scored on Worksheet 1, someone checks it for risk, and an answer comes back within a set number of days.

| Box | Needed in the lab? | What goes in it | Example | Your answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Risk check by** | Optional | Who looks at a new AI idea for risk before it is approved: one person or a small group | The review group (Box 6) | |
| **Target turnaround (days)** | Optional | How many days someone with a new AI idea should wait for a yes or a no | 5 | |

### Back at work: Box 4 — Vendor checks

The questions you will put to every company that sells you an AI tool. Tick the ones you want on your standard list.

- ☐ Do you train on our data?
- ☐ Where is our data stored?
- ☐ How do we leave? (how we get our data out and stop using the tool)
- ☐ Do you support MCP / standard integration? (MCP is the common standard for connecting AI tools to your systems)

### Back at work: Box 5 — Incident path

An incident is AI going wrong: it leaks information, makes something up that gets used, or takes a wrong action. The incident path says who gets the first call.

| Box | Needed in the lab? | What goes in it | Example | Your answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **First call goes to** | Optional | The one person or desk anyone can call the moment AI leaks, makes things up or misfires | The IT help desk | |
| **Rehearsed on (date)** | Optional | The date you will walk through a made-up incident to test the path | February 12, 2027 | |

### Back at work: Box 6 — Roles

Who looks after AI use: a named owner for each use, plus a small review group that approves new AI ideas and keeps this playbook up to date.

| Box | Needed in the lab? | What goes in it | Example | Your answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Review group** | Optional | Three to five people from different parts of the organization: job titles are enough | Dana Ruiz (Support Ops), plus one person each from IT, Finance and HR | |

### Back at work: Box 7 — Audit trail

The audit trail is the record of what your AI tools did, kept so that someone can check afterwards: what gets logged, who reviews it, and how long the logs are kept.

| Box | Needed in the lab? | What goes in it | Example | Your answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What gets logged** | Optional | What each AI tool records about its own use: for example who used it, when, and what action it took | Who used the helpdesk add-on, on which ticket, and whether its draft was sent or changed | |
| **Who reviews the logs, and how often** | Optional | The person or group that looks at those records, and how often | The support operations manager, on the first Monday of each month | |
| **How long logs are kept** | Optional | How long the records are kept before they are deleted | 12 months | |
