Lab 5, step 3The five criteria

List three candidates, then score each 1–5 on every criterion. Higher is better. Risk is scored in reverse: 5 = low risk.

Value — Which business number it improves (time, cost, quality or revenue), and whether the budget owner cares about that number. 5: A number the budget owner is measured on moves · 1: “It would be innovative”
Data readiness — Whether the data it needs exists, is clean, and is yours to use. 5: Data exists, is clean, and we may use it · 1: Data is scattered, dirty, or legally unclear
Feasibility — How easy it is to put in place: can a bought tool do it, and can one team own it? 5: A bought tool does it, or one team can own it end to end · 1: Needs custom build + three teams + new data pipelines
Risk (5 = low) — What happens when it is wrong, and what sensitive data it touches. 5: Internal, human-reviewed, reversible · 1: Customer-facing, regulated, irreversible
Measurability — Whether there is a “before” number today, and a result you can see within 90 days. 5: Baseline exists; result visible in 90 days · 1: No baseline; benefits “strategic”

Lab 5, steps 2–5Candidates Required

Three candidates, each with all five scores. Name each one as a workflow: “triage inbound support tickets,” not “use AI in support.”

First-pilot rule: a first pilot needs 4+ on Risk and 4+ on Measurability, whatever its total.

Quick version, for judging one proposal someone brings you: ask four questions instead of five (fold Data readiness into Feasibility). The rule is the same: Risk and Measurability must pass, however good the Value story is.

Lab 6, steps 8–9Finance red-team findings

A red-team is a deliberate attack on your own plan, to find its weak spots before someone else does. In step 8 an AI reviewer plays a skeptic from finance and names three weak spots, each with a fix. In step 9 you write down the two that matter most.

“Gameable” means the primary metric could look better without the work getting better. Write the weak spot the AI reviewer found in your plan, and how you will close it.

Would the primary metric have improved anyway, without the AI? Write why it might, and how you will tell the difference.

Lab 7, step 8Box 1 — Inventory (where is AI used today?)

Required: at least two tools. List two or three AI tools already in use where you work. For each one, give the tool and what it is used for, its owner (the one person answerable for it; “nobody yet” is a real answer), and the data it touches (what information it can see). Autonomy tier is how much the tool does on its own. 1 assist: AI drafts, a person does the work. 2 review: AI prepares the action, a named person approves it. 3 autonomous: AI acts, and people check afterwards.
